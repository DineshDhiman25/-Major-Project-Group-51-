The ongoing conflict in Ukraine and Russia is an example that highlights the role that social media can play as a channel for the dissemination of unfiltered information in real-time from conflict zones. In an environment where traditional media is heavily restricted and information warfare is prevalent, social media platforms have become essential tools for event documentation in conflict zones. This is especially the case for anonymous media platforms, which have unique potential for monitoring and identifying possible human rights violations.

In this paper, we present an analysis of large amounts of data gathered through the Telegram platform, which is a major platform for consuming independent news in the post-Soviet region. We curate a large corpus of posts from public Telegram channels focused on politics and war news. We then use this corpus for analysis to identify any possible human rights violations. We also present an automated method for identifying human rights violations in Telegram posts using a text classification method based on a language model. This method achieves a high increase in performance compared to multilingual language models for the task and achieves an F2 score of 0.71.

To further aid in future research in this direction, two datasets are also presented in this paper. One is a large-scale dataset consisting of over 1.6 million Telegram posts, and the other is a sentence-level annotated dataset that indicates the presence or absence of human rights violations in the text. The context for this research is the ongoing war in Ukraine and Russia.
